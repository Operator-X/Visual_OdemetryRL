## Abstract (draft, to revise once the final experiments are in)
Reinforcement learning (RL) has been proposed to replace the hand-tuned heuristics inside visual odometry (VO), such as
keyframe selection [Messikommer et al., ECCV 2024]. We replicate this approach for SVO natively on a laptop and find
that, at a reduced training scale, the RL agent matches the robustness of SVO's tuned rules with fewer keyframes but
not their accuracy. We diagnose why: the reward is dominated by scene difficulty (99.7% of steps receive a positive
reward), it measures local rather than accumulated error, and the benefit of a keyframe arrives several frames after
the decision. We further find that reward modifications reliably drive the keyframe rate to an extreme (none or
every frame), and we report a bug in the reference implementation and a weakness of the standard evaluation metric. We
then study fixes: warm-starting from a behavior clone of SVO's own rules, constrained RL with a learned keyframe
price, residual RL, and a new rule-relative reward that compares the agent against a shadow copy of SVO running its
rules on the same images. [Final claims depend on the remaining experiments.]

## Contributions (current, to be confirmed)
1. **Replication at small scale** (native Apple Silicon build, ~1.6% of the paper's training steps): what reproduces
   (efficiency: robustness with fewer keyframes; SVO baseline within 8% of the paper) and what does not (the accuracy gain).
2. **Diagnosis of the RL signal:** weak (difficulty-dominated), local (5-frame window vs trajectory drift), delayed
   (keyframe benefit after 4-5 frames, discounted away at gamma=0.6). Quantified with `scripts/reward_diagnosis.py`.
3. **Keyframe-collapse phenomenon:** across every reward change the keyframe rate slides to an extreme; the keyframe
   penalty weight decides which. Also the mechanism behind sim-to-real failures.
4. **Methodological issues found:** a GT re-initialization bug in the reference code (failure counts inflated ~11x);
   the first-segment ATE can be gamed by failing early (we report coverage-aware metrics and "finished" counts).
5. **Fixes studied (comparative):** behavior-cloning warm start (most accurate on TUM, -4% vs rules, 3 seeds; less
   robust on TartanAir), constrained PPO with a PI-controlled Lagrangian keyframe price (matches the tuned rules on TUM,
   most robust on TartanAir, 3 seeds), residual RL (collapses to the rules), and the **shadow-SVO rule-relative reward**
   (new; beats the rules on training data, most robust, but by adding keyframes).
